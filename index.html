2. What our service does

We provide a social media publishing and reporting service. It lets us, and our business clients, do two things on the clients' own social media pages:

  1. Publish. Create, schedule and publish posts to a Page or account that the client owns.
  2. Report. Read performance figures for that Page or account, such as follower counts, impressions and engagement on posts, so the client can see how its content is performing.

We run a separate, self-hosted copy of the open-source scheduling tool Postiz (licensed under AGPL-3.0) for each client. Each copy is isolated: it has its own database, storage and access key. We do not mix one client's data with another's.

We connect to LinkedIn and, later, Meta (Facebook and Instagram) and X, using developer apps that Corda Intelligence registers and owns on each platform. The service is not offered to the public, and people cannot sign up for it on their own. A post is never published until a person has approved it.

The service connects only to Pages and accounts that a person with the right authority has chosen to connect (see section 5).

3. What information we collect

When a Page or account is connected, and when the service is used, we collect and store:

  • Access tokens. The access and refresh tokens that a platform issues when an authorised person connects a Page. These let the service post to, and read figures from, that Page.
  • Profile and Page details. Basic details the platform returns about the connected Page and the person who connected it. This may include a name, Page or profile ID, logo or profile picture, and Page or organisation name.
  • Post content and media. The text, images, videos and links that we or the client write or upload for publishing, plus scheduling details (for example, the planned time and the target Page).
  • Performance figures. Counts and trends the platform returns for a connected Page, such as followers, impressions, reactions and comments on posts. These are mainly totals. Where a platform includes the name of a person who reacted or commented, we do not collect that detail for any purpose beyond showing the client its own Page performance.
  • Approval records. Who approved a post and when.
  • Basic logs. Technical records such as timestamps, IP addresses, request and error logs, and records of when Pages are connected, disconnected or posted to. These are used to operate and secure the service.

Post content or media can sometimes include personal information about other people, for example a person shown in a photo or named in a post. We include this only where the client has a proper basis to publish it.

We do not use the service to build databases of LinkedIn members, followers or other users. We do not scrape or profile people. We do not read private messages or connection lists. We request only the platform permissions needed to publish and to read Page performance.

4. Why we collect it

We use this information only to:

  • authenticate with each platform and keep the connection active;
  • create, schedule and publish approved posts to the connected Page;
  • show the client the status of its posts and the performance of its Page;
  • keep the service working, secure and free of errors.

We do not use data received from LinkedIn, Meta or X for advertising, profiling, selling, or any purpose other than publishing to, and reporting on, the connected Page for the client who connected it.

5. Whose information, and who can connect a Page

Each client is responsible for its own Page and the content on it. A client's Page is connected only when a person who holds an administrator role on that Page signs in to the platform and approves the connection. We never ask for, and never hold, a client's platform password.

We act on a client's instructions and under a written agreement with that client. That agreement requires us to handle personal information as if the Privacy Act applied to us, to keep each client's data separate, to notify the client promptly of a breach affecting its data, and to delete its data when the engagement ends.

6. How data from LinkedIn and other platforms is used

  • Data received through the LinkedIn API (including the Community Management API) is used only to publish to, and report on, the Page that the client connected, as authorised by the person who connected it.
  • We request only the permissions (scopes) needed for that purpose.
  • We do not sell, rent, license or share LinkedIn member data. We do not combine it with other data sets or use it to identify, target or contact members.
  • The same rules apply to data received from Meta and X.

7. We do not sell your data

We do not sell, rent or trade personal information to anyone.

8. Who we share it with

We disclose information only as needed to run the service:

  • LinkedIn (LinkedIn Corporation / LinkedIn Ireland), to authenticate, publish and read Page figures. Privacy policy: https://www.linkedin.com/legal/privacy-policy
  • Meta (Meta Platforms, Inc. / Meta Platforms Ireland Ltd), when connected. Privacy policy: https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy
  • X (X Corp.), when connected. Privacy policy: https://x.com/en/privacy
  • Hosting provider: Amazon Web Services (Amazon Lightsail), Sydney, Australia. Each client's server, database and uploaded media sit on a virtual machine there. Its disk and backups are encrypted by AWS.
  • The client whose Page is connected, who can see the posts, media and performance figures for its own Page.

When a post is published, its content becomes public on that platform and is handled under that platform's own terms and privacy policy.

We may also disclose information where required or authorised by law.

Overseas disclosure (APP 8)

LinkedIn, Meta and X are based outside Australia, mainly in the United States and Ireland. Information sent to them to authenticate, publish and read figures is handled overseas.

Our servers, uploaded media and backups are located in Sydney, Australia. Amazon Web Services is part of a group based in the United States. Its terms say it will not move our data outside the Sydney region unless that is needed to provide the service or the law requires it.

9. How long we keep it and how it is deleted

  • Access tokens are kept only while a Page is connected. When a Page is disconnected, we remove the connection and revoke our app on the platform, so the token stops working. Any stored copy is deleted with the rest of the client's data when the engagement ends.
  • Post content and media are kept while the client is engaged with us, for our publishing records. They are deleted when the engagement ends.
  • Performance figures are shown to the client from the platform when requested. Where we keep summary figures for reporting, they are kept while the engagement lasts and deleted with the rest of the client's data.
  • Logs are kept for 90 days and then deleted.
  • Backups are encrypted and deleted after 30 days.
  • When a client leaves, we disconnect every Page, revoke the connection on the platform side, and delete that client's servers, storage and backups.

Deleting data from our service does not remove posts already published on a platform. Those must be deleted on the platform itself.

How to revoke access

Anyone who has connected a Page or account can revoke our access at any time:

  • LinkedIn: Settings & Privacy → Data privacy → "Permitted services" (https://www.linkedin.com/psettings/permitted-services), then remove the app.
  • Meta (Facebook): Settings & privacy → Settings → "Business integrations" or "Apps and websites", then remove the app.
  • X: Settings and privacy → Security and account access → "Apps and sessions" → "Connected apps", then revoke access.

After access is revoked, the service can no longer post to or read from that Page. The stored token stops working and we delete it.

Deletion requests

You can ask us to delete personal information we hold about you by emailing isaac@cordaintel.com. Tell us the name or Page the information relates to. We will respond within 30 days.

10. How we keep it secure

We take reasonable steps to protect information from misuse, interference, loss, and unauthorised access, change or disclosure. These include:

  • encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS) to the service and to each platform;
  • an encrypted server disk, and encrypted backups, for every client's server;
  • a database that is not reachable from the internet;
  • a separate server, database, storage and access key for each client;
  • access limited to Corda and the client's authorised users;
  • a human approval step before any post is published;
  • requesting only the platform permissions needed to publish and report;
  • keeping the server software up to date, and rotating secrets on a schedule and after any suspected leak.

No system is completely secure. If a data breach is likely to cause serious harm, we will notify affected people and the OAIC where the law requires it, and we will tell the affected client promptly.
